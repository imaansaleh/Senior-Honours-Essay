# The Role of Digital Access and Skills in Financial Service Use in Canada: Evidence from the Canadian Internet Use Survey – Public Use Microdata File

**Senior Honours Essay | Economics | University of Waterloo | 2026**

## Overview

This paper examines how digital access and digital skills are associated with the use of digital financial services in Canada and whether these relationships vary across socio-demographic groups.

Using the 2022 Canadian Internet Use Survey (CIUS) Public Use Microdata File, the analysis examines four forms of digital financial service use: online purchasing, online banking, online investing, and online tax filing.

## Data & Methodology

**Data:** 2022 Canadian Internet Use Survey (CIUS) Public Use Microdata File  
**Sample:** 21,603 observations  
**Methodology:** Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) / Linear Probability Models and interaction analysis

## Key Findings

Digital skills are positively associated with digital financial service use, with spreadsheet skills particularly relevant for online investing and tax filing.

Smartphone access is strongly associated with routine digital financial activities such as online purchasing and banking.

Income, education, gender, age, and geography remain associated with differences in digital financial participation after accounting for digital access and skills.
